## Risks and follow-up

- **Known risks:**
  - **Licence:**
    - RTMDet-tiny (the recommended model), EfficientDet-Lite and RT-DETR weights are **licence unconfirmed**: their code is Apache-2.0, but the weight files have no licence statement of their own.
    - YOLOX and RF-DETR-Nano weights are Apache-2.0.
    - Under the owner's free-licence rule, RTMDet needs written confirmation before use.
  - **CPU:**
    - RTMDet-tiny at 960 costs 620 ms per frame on one thread, about 1.2 cores per gate at 2 fps.
    - P1 jobs at 4-6 cores starved the live streams on 2026-10-02 (8 to 1.5 fps).
    - P2 must measure stream fps in shadow.
  - **Little footage:** 8.2 min of real entry video, so false alarms/h is only bounded (< about 7/h). Clutter changes daily: two new clips moved YOLOX-Nano's threshold from 0.20 to 0.55.
  - **Workers stay in view:** with the whole picture, the 3 s rule and all hours, the standing workers behind the cages will trigger repeated alerts during working hours.
  - **Biased ground truth:** it is pooled from the candidates plus Grounding DINO, so a person missed by all of them is missing from the truth too.
- **Unresolved questions:**
  1. Can the owner or legal confirm that OpenMMLab's RTMDet-tiny COCO weights may be used commercially (Apache-2.0)?
  2. Is about 1.2 cores per gate acceptable for the presence thread? If not, start with YOLOX-Nano 960 at 0.55.
  3. INT's 12:46:50Z clip: the face shadow engine logged 3 person tracks, but nobody is visible. Possibly false face tracks, e.g. a bag with printed cartoon faces on the bench. Please check the stored crops.
- **Dependencies on other agents/commits:** none. INT exported the footage.
- **Requested integration action/order:**
  1. Review and merge `eval/presence-p1` (docs + tools only; no hotspot).
  2. Get the RTMDet weight licence confirmed (or decide on YOLOX-Nano).
  3. Export the footage listed in report section 8: continuous working-hours and evening hours, lights-off video, early-morning or night people, the same for EXIT.
